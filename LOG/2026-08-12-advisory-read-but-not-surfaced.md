# [HASH] — The forward advisory is surfaced on its own line before everything else, because it was the easiest thing in a crowded opening to drop

/plan's Step 1 required the advisory to be read and surfaced in one line as orientation. One session read it correctly and said nothing about it for three hours; it came up only because the session ran out of obvious next items and went looking. The work it named was eventually done, so nothing was lost — but the mechanism did not do that, luck did. The advisory exists so a recommendation survives between sessions without depending on anyone's memory, and here it failed inside the mechanism designed to prevent exactly that failure.

The diagnosis is placement. Step 1 folded the advisory into a consolidated opening narration alongside the below-line revisit, the mail check, the throughput floor and the housekeeping. A one-line orientation is the easiest thing in that message to drop, and nothing downstream ever checks it was said. It now goes first and alone, before the consolidated opening; the consolidate-the-scans rule still governs everything after it.

The limit is written into the step rather than implied: this makes the line harder to drop, not impossible, and nothing will ever confirm it was said. The build must not describe it as fixing the problem.

The required-artifact route — this project's usual reach — was rejected for a specific reason worth keeping. The rule-gate disposition works because its artifact lands in a committed file a later reader or a script can check. There is no equivalent here: the advisory is cleared at the close, and a "the advisory was surfaced" line in the LOG would be Claude attesting to its own narration, which verifies nothing. A check that cannot fail is worse than no check, because it reads as coverage.

Counter-evidence, recorded so the fix is not oversold: the /plan that processed this item **did** surface the advisory in the old placement, and it worked — it is why one item was processed second. The existing placement does not fail every time. It was changed anyway because "it worked this time" is the reasoning that nearly saved a requirement two items earlier in that same session, and the change is cheap.

One adjacent correction rode along, caught by editing the surrounding paragraph rather than by its own item: the index-read step still asserted that an index line "is capped at a share of its entry, so the read is bounded" — a cap repealed two items earlier in this same run. Reworded to the surviving requirement.
