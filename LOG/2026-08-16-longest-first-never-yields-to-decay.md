# d82f538 — The longest-first rung is partitioned at the median so it terminates, and decay by date filed sits beneath it

Raised by the user and processed in the same conversation, in her words: losing decay control by proxy of file order was wrong; line count size is so important, but line count will run interminably whereas blockingness doesn't, it runs out.

**The structural fault is sharper than the ladder's own account of itself.** Rungs 1 and 2 are **selectors** — an uncleared red flag, and items other work cites — so each picks a subset and runs out. Rung 3, longest-first, was a **total order**: it ranked every entry, so it never yielded. `plan.md` presented that as a feature, saying nothing could sit beneath it. What it actually meant is that **any rung below longest-first could never fire**. That is the siteless-mechanism failure this project has recorded repeatedly, sitting in a place nobody looked for it. The sentence claiming totality was deleted rather than reworded, because it is what made the fault invisible.

**Why decay was cut, corrected from the record rather than from the account of it.** The log frames its deletion as a decision that staleness stops being an ordering concern. That was not the reason. Her reason was that she could not think on the spot of a way to terminate the line-count rung, and judged that rung more important — which she still does. So the deletion was a forced choice under a constraint now removed, not a finding that decay does not belong.

**Decay reads the DATE FILED, not file order, and that correction is hers.** A user can ask for the queue to be reordered however they like, which would silently render a file-order decay rung useless — it would still run, ranking by something just overwritten. The date an entry first appeared cannot be overwritten by a reorder, and the digest already computes it. The robust version is also the more mechanical one.

**The termination takes no arbitrary number.** A bare figure is banned; a proportion of the thing it governs is expressly admissible. The partition is the section's median line count, computed at the opening and printed by the digest. Longest-first orders only the above-median group, which is finite and shrinks as it is worked, so the rung genuinely yields and what sits beneath it becomes reachable for the first time. No number is written into the method's text.

**One claim deliberately NOT carried into the rule.** She observed that a long item can hint at accumulated thought or research. That may be true and must not become part of the justification: longest-first rests on cost-of-reading precisely because length does **not** predict readiness — in the session that settled it, the two longest of eight were the two that could not be kept at all.
