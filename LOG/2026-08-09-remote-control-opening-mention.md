# [HASH] — The stored working-mode field retired and replaced by one rendering default plus a bundled one-line inline-text offer in the session's opening narration

The user asked for this on 2026-08-09, near-verbatim: *"let me know if you're on remote control and I'll present text in-line for your approval."* The design is Claude's, settled with the user's agreement in the same session. Two parts of their wording were deliberately changed, and both are named here rather than quietly altered.

Stripped to its substance, what they wanted was one mention at the start of every session, folded into the existing opening narration rather than asked as its own question — a standing offer, not a prompt that waits for an answer. Bundled is the whole point: a standalone session-start question is exactly the shape the method has spent weeks removing, and the consolidate-the-scans rule already governs this moment. One clause inside a narration that already fires costs nothing; one more question at every opening is the nag pattern returning by a new route.

The stored field went, and the evidence rather than a preference decided it. Working mode was a persistent field in the project's CLAUDE.md, set once at setup, existing specifically so there would be no session-start ask. `0ad5fa9` retired it pre-revert on a measured finding recorded in `[queue-items-on-falsified-premises]`: the field asked *where the user is*, and its one measured user answered `local` regardless of where they actually were — so it recorded reading appetite, not location. A field that measures the wrong thing and needs a setup question, a template field, an unset-field ask and a per-session flip to maintain is worse than one default plus an offer. The net is subtraction: a stored field, a setup question, a template field and the unset-field ask all removed, one clause added.

First change to the user's wording — it governs the report, not the approval. `[write-first-approval-flow]`, built earlier in this same run, removes the pasted-draft-for-approval step entirely, so "present text in-line for your approval" would have offered something the method no longer does. What a remote user actually needs is the *report after the write* to carry the text inline instead of a pointer they cannot conveniently open on a phone. Same benefit, correct mechanism — and this is why the two items were sequenced in that order.

Second change — "remote control" is the user's term for their own setup, not a term to ship. In the consumer-facing clause the offer describes the situation rather than naming a feature: reading on a phone, away from the computer, wherever opening a file is awkward. A consumer who has never driven a session remotely should still recognise themselves in it.

Scope was held to the session's first opening narration only, not repeated at every skill invocation. The user's own phrasing was "start of every session," and repeating it per skill would rebuild the nag this design exists to avoid.

This makes `[working-mode-render-verification]` moot — that below-line `[user]` item verifies the mechanism now retired, and `[queue-items-on-falsified-premises]` had already flagged it as resting on a falsified premise. There is nothing left for it to verify.

Built together with `[doc-link-rendering-settled]`, which retires the Editor field: with working mode gone, the recorded editor was the last remaining gate on whether a pointer was emitted at all, so the two are halves of one change and share this run's files.
