The blocker this clears is the derivation. A band cannot honestly be read off a bloated corpus — the measurement script says so in its own docstring — but July can be read, because it precedes the August doubling. Each band's top is July's median for that shape, its floor half of that, its ceiling one and a half times it. Every number is a proportion of a measured figure, which is what the derivation rule admits; the three multipliers are the single judgment, adjustable without redoing the derivation.

Two shipped texts forbade what this ships, and both were reconciled rather than overridden. The script's docstring banned a "too long" column outright; `done.md` banned restoring the repealed 20% index-line cap "or another figure". Read closely, each ban is against a threshold read off the bloated middle — the docstring says so explicitly — and against a *proportional* figure in `done.md`'s case, since proportionality is what made the old cap fire on short entries rather than long lines. Bands from July breach neither. Both texts were reworded to say what they actually mean, so a later session does not read them as forbidding what now exists.

The eviction is the authoring standard's completeness-over-compression clause, which authorised unbounded growth and is replaced by the bands. A floor clause ships alongside them so the ceiling cannot be read as a target: a record too thin to rebuild intent from fails as surely as one too long to get through, and a short shape is read for what is missing rather than padded to reach a number.

The breach checks ride reads that already happen — the scrub read at `/plan`'s keep-step, and the scrub read at the LOG-entry step — rather than adding passes of their own. Both are advisory: a breach names an action and never blocks a write.
