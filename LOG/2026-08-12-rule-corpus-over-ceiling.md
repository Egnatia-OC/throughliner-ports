# e5d169b — The eviction pass runs on relevance rather than a count, with a disposition per change

This item was filed by the board's MEASURED signal as "get the count under 200". Both the count and the ceiling are gone — the 150–200 figure was re-validated against the 5-series this same session and found roughly an order of magnitude too tight — so the item was re-aimed at the user's decision into a **relevance-driven** pass: remove text irrelevant to the session reading it, or over-prescriptive for a 5-series model. The slug keeps its old name because slugs are immutable; the prose governs.

Losing the number costs less than it first appears, because the surviving case never rested on it: irrelevant content degrades the model's treatment of *all* instructions rather than being neatly skipped, near-identical rules are optimal distractors for one another, and Anthropic's own 5-series guidance is to remove prior-model scaffolding. None of those is something a count measures. But it does mean no number can judge the pass, so the requirement to state a disposition per change — previously a caution against gaming a counter — is promoted to the completion criterion.

**The four dispositions:**

1. **The write-first block's authoring rationale — relocate-rationale.** Two passages cut: why the test was reworded from "does a revert fully undo it?", and why it needed no third exception. The purpose-clause test was applied by deleting each and reading what remains; the operative test and its table stand complete without either. The QUEUE.md-conversion case was *kept*, recast to five lines as a worked case, because it is the one that keeps being re-argued and cutting it would invite the re-argument.

2. **next.md's uncleared-flag backstop — KEEP, and the judgment is the deliverable.** This was the one entry the item said was a decision rather than an edit, and the decision is to keep. It also records that the backstop *was* once missing while SPEC promised it. That makes it operative text, not history. Recasting was weighed and refused — shortening it keeps the words and loses the deterrent.

This *is* redistribution, which the compliance checklist warns is how bloat gets hidden, and it is legitimate here only because the trigger is unmissable — Claude is about to touch a physical device — and because it merged into a rule on the same subject rather than becoming a new section.

4. **The two file-paging texts — consolidate-and-repeal, general case preserved.** The always-loaded rule keeps the obligation and its why, now stated once for any file rather than for QUEUE.md specifically, and absorbs one sentence saying a code-generated digest satisfies it more strongly. `plan.md` no longer restates that why. The item's caution was right: a straight repeal at the always-loaded site would have lost the general case, which is why the survivor lives there rather than at the site that acts on it.
