The session's dominant finding is the same one the previous close reported, at a higher rate: **seven of the fifteen items worked here rested on a claim that a one-minute check falsified.** The previous close found eight of sixteen. Two consecutive sessions at roughly that rate stops being an anecdote about a few stale captures and starts being a property of the queue: an item is written when its claims are true and then sits while the project moves under it, and nothing re-reads it until processing. The processing-time claim check is the only thing catching this, it caught seven, and in four cases it reversed the outcome rather than merely correcting a detail.

The seven, because the pattern is more instructive than any one of them:

- **[statusline-context-reader]** — its own lift-condition had named the deleting outcome in advance, and the probe armed last session delivered it. The desktop app **silently ignores the `statusLine` setting** (anthropics/claude-code#41456, open since 2026-03-31, no official response; the CLI does support it). Verified three ways rather than assumed: the script ran correctly on a hand-fed payload, was registered at a valid settings path, and produced neither its marker file nor its status-bar text across a full quit-and-relaunch. Deleted, with the probe and the setting. **The consequence is larger than one item: Claude cannot see its own context level in this app by any available route**, since hooks receive no context data either — so every automatic wrap-before-you-run-out design is off the table until the surface changes.
- **[no-standard-names-for-workflow-docs-vs-user-files]** — the capture said "project docs" and "method docs" denote the same set. They denote *different* sets, differing on SPEC.md: `pre_tool_use.py` excludes SPEC from "method docs" deliberately, which is the containment stopping a build rewriting the project's own definition. So this was never a naming inelegance; it is a **wrong description of a safety boundary**. The design changed accordingly — in the scope rule, enumerate the set rather than name it, because a name always admits a wrong reading and a list has none.

**The session's other substantial output is a published contract.** The user's mdreader project — a Markdown reader for Windows, revived and now an explicit companion to this one — needs to know when Claude is writing so its user's typing and Claude's edits don't collide. They rejected inferring it from file-change timing, correctly: an app can see *that* a file changed but not *who* changed it. [editing-state-signal-for-companion-apps] was designed in full at their request and cleared at the top of Processed. The core decision is that the signal is a **heartbeat, not a lock** — a plain on/off flag left stuck on by a crashed session would lock the user out of their own document permanently, which is the exact harm the timing-guess approach was rejected for, reintroduced by the fix. Per-session marker files at `<project-root>/.throughliner/editing-<session-id>.json`, carrying a version field, an active flag, a refreshed timestamp and the target paths; fail-open, never committed, and the reader owns the timeout policy because the hook cannot know what the user is doing. The honest limit is stated in the item: it can say a write is in flight, which files, and how long it has been quiet — it cannot say Claude has finished rather than paused, and nothing available can.
