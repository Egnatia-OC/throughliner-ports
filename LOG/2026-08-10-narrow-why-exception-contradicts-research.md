# [HASH] — The gate's narrow why-clause exception repealed and replaced by the purpose-clause test

The repeal is the user's, in their own words captured earlier: this is not
consistent with our research in law, and we do not want why-clauses accumulating.
The replacement mechanism was built across one exchange in which the user defeated
three successive Claude proposals; each defeat is recorded in
`resources/self-authoring-rules.md` itself, because each is a shape a later
session will reach for again.

## What was repealed, and why a bare repeal was not enough

Gone from `resources/self-authoring-rules.md`: *"The narrow exception, by
exception and not by default: a rule whose reason is genuinely needed to apply it
correctly at its edges keeps one short clause."* The same sentence was repeated at
`SPEC.md` line 54 and is gone from there too.

The user's argument: the section derives from legislation, which states the
binding rule bare and puts reasoning in material published alongside, with no
"unless the reason helps" carve-out — and an exception phrased as a judgment is
decided by whoever is authoring, every one of whom believes their own rule is the
edge case. That is the shape under which the predecessor document grew from 6,162
to 21,445 words while being honestly applied.

But some sentences genuinely must travel with a rule for it to be applied
correctly, and repealing with nothing in place strips them. That half was missing
from the original capture and is what this build supplies.

## The replacement: reclassification, not exemption

If a reason is needed to apply the rule correctly, it is not rationale attached to
the rule — it is *part of* the rule, written as operative text. Law does this as a
purpose or objects clause. Reclassification asks a question with an answer — can
this rule be applied correctly without this sentence? — where the repealed
exception asked how special a rule is.

Three parts, and **the order is the safeguard**: admission on recorded evidence of
misapplication; protection by grammar, welding it into the operative sentence so
removal leaves the rule visibly incomplete; review from the drafting note, which
cites the instance so a later auditor can re-test it.

The instance never ships. What ships is the purpose clause alone, so the shipped
docs get *shorter* than under the repealed exception, which licensed a short
clause of reasoning where this licenses none.

`resources/method-compliance-audit-checklist.md` gained one line naming purpose
clauses as a non-target, so a deliberate eviction sweep is told before it starts.

## The honest limit

This protects against accidental stripping, which is the failure that has actually
happened. It cannot stop a pass that deliberately rewrites a rule to remove the
dependency; nothing short of a hook that understands meaning could. What the form
guarantees is that doing so is a visible rewrite rather than a quiet deletion.
