# b51d205 — CLAUDE.md's Discord section gains a purpose statement: announce Throughliner changes and nothing else

Written in the tail after the 2026-08-12 planning close, at the user's request and in response to a failure minutes earlier.

**What happened.** Asked whether the planning session had anything worth a Discord post, Claude drafted three: Claude Code's worktree-removal behaviour, GitHub's rename-redirect break condition, and two permission-rule traps. All three were accurate, sourced and useful. None belonged in the channel. The user's correction, in their own words: they feel completely irrelevant to the workflow and user experience, and the purpose of the posts at the moment is to announce new features, changes or improvements to Throughliner — not tips on how to use Claude Code or Throughliner.

**The diagnosis, which is Claude's and is what the new clause encodes.** The section already said to announce only what has shipped, and Claude satisfied that rule perfectly — everything drafted was true of something installed. What was missing was any statement of *subject*. With the shipped-only test passed and no subject test to fail, three off-topic posts read as compliant. The pull is specific: a session that has just *learned* something experiences it as the session's output, so findings feel announceable. The operative test now written into the rule — "did we learn it" versus "did Throughliner change" — is the one that separates them.

**The clause also states the consequence the session was avoiding**: a planning session has nothing to announce, however much it decided. Reaching for a substitute subject is exactly what happened here, and saying so plainly is cheaper than a session inventing one each time.

**Gate outcome on the wording, recorded because the rule was applied to itself.** The first draft carried the failure story inside the operative text — three sentences of anecdote about what Claude drafted and why. That is rationale riding a rule, which `resources/self-authoring-rules.md` places in the deciding LOG entry rather than in the rule. It was trimmed to the operative test, and the story lives here. The purpose-clause check was applied in reverse to confirm the trim was right: delete the anecdote and the rule still reads as a complete instruction, which means it was rationale.

**One thing noted and not acted on.** The whole Discord section is always-loaded in this project while its trigger is a word the user says, which is the shape the distribution test says can be fetched on demand. Moving it is a larger change than this clause warrants and would be scoped as its own work; recording it here so the observation is not lost.
