# 02ec308 — one outgoing index absorbs three separate absences, and the mailbox-versus-captures question is refused

Five mailbox captures were taken as one group at the user's direction. They split three ways rather than one.

**Three of them were the same absence: nothing here records what leaves.** No destination or intent on a send, no sent copy anywhere in a mailbox whose archive holds twenty-two inbound messages and nothing outbound, and no record of what was ever announced — so a shipped change falsifies a past post and nothing notices. The live instance arrived in the same conversation: the user's spec-driven-development post was inverted by a decision taken minutes earlier. One mechanism answers all three, and it is an index rather than a second copy of anything: `INBOX/sent.md`, one line per outbound artifact carrying date, destination, intent and what was claimed, written in the same turn as the approved send. It sits inside `INBOX/` because that folder is never committed and the lines name other projects — the same reason the address book lives there, with the same stated cost.

Her clearing rule then gains teeth: a send may clear a work item only where the record says it was handed over **for completion**.

**The mail-versus-direct-captures question was refused, using the principle she had insisted on an hour earlier.** A capture written straight into another project's queue is one project's Claude putting work into another's queue without that project's planning session agreeing it — the same missing crossing as a build certifying its own SPEC sentence. Mail forces the crossing. It would also need cross-project blockers, sacrificing the lint's slug validation to remove a mechanism whose cost had just fallen twice.
