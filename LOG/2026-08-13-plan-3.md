# 6268c71 — Researched what actually shortens Claude's responses, and cleared five items to build from it

Alex opened the session by prioritising something she had been putting off because she
did not know whether it was possible: she is fatigued by very long Claude responses and
believes they could be shorter. She asked for research on two things she named
separately — the most effective rules for stopping Claude bundling, and, distinctly,
for delivering detail less painfully. Keeping those two apart was her framing, and it
turned out to be the right cut, though not along the line either of us expected.

The research is filed at
[`resources/research/response-length-and-bundling-steering.md`](../resources/research/response-length-and-bundling-steering.md).
Its headline is that the problem she described as one thing is two, and the split is
not bundling-versus-length but **length versus narration**. Anthropic's Opus 5 guide
gives them separate sections and separate fixes: response length is how long a single
message is; progress narration is how often the model speaks at all during agentic work
— announcing before each tool call, updating mid-task, summarising after. In a Claude
Code session most of the text a user reads is the second, and the shipped output style
addresses only the first. That gap is the largest thing this session found, and the
official countermeasure describes a *cadence* rather than asking for brevity, which is
what makes it actionable where an adjective is not.

Two confirmed dead ends are recorded so they are not tried. The effort parameter does
not shorten visible output — Opus 5 is named in the docs as the exception, where raising
or lowering effort does not reliably change response length. And restating the rule more
often does not work: the one-at-a-time rule is currently asserted in three layers
(global CLAUDE.md, project CLAUDE.md, the output style) and still slips, which is local
evidence for the official line that volume is not the missing ingredient.

On bundling specifically, the honest finding is that **no lever is documented at all**.
Anthropic's guidance says nothing about releasing output one piece at a time, and the
general LLM literature that surfaces on the phrasing is prompt-chaining research — about
how a *human* should decompose their prompts to improve model accuracy, which is a
different problem in the opposite direction and is recorded in the research file as
something not to cite in support. What the evidence does support is a shown specimen:
examples are called one of the most reliable ways to steer output structure, and the
local evidence is stronger than the citation, because `plan.md`'s checkpoint is the one
site in this method where bundling was actually fixed and it was fixed by showing the
message and then stating flatly what does not appear beneath it.

Two items were designed in-session rather than deferred, and both rejected the intuitive
answer. The concision reminder goes at the end of the session-start hook's injected text
rather than in the four skill command prompts: the prompts sit genuinely later in the
load order, which is a real advantage given up deliberately, but the same line would then
be maintained in four places in the layer this project is separately trying to shrink,
and Anthropic's wording asks for a reminder "near the end" rather than literally last.
Alex chose the hook in her own words. The bundling item was filed proposing a transcript
audit to locate the worst-bundling sites, and that was argued down at processing on
Claude's recommendation: the audit is the rigorous path but spends heavily to find sites
when the fix is probably generic, so one specimen goes into the output style first and
the audit is held as the follow-on, with a concrete failure to point it at if it comes to
that. It is recorded as held back, not refused.

One item was deleted, and its deletion produced the session's most useful constraint.
[markdown-density-may-be-half-the-fatigue] asked whether heavy bolding and bulleting was
part of what reads as "long" — Anthropic ships a ready-made block for suppressing
markdown, on the reasoning that bullets fragment information. That was filed as a
question rather than acted on, because fatigue at length and fatigue at density feel
identical from inside and have opposite fixes, and because Alex's low symbol-search score
argues that dense lead-ins are how she finds the point at all. Asked directly, her answer
was that the formatting helps. The item was deleted and the answer written into the
research file as a constraint binding the four remaining builds: cut length and narration
frequency, leave formatting density alone. Anthropic's markdown-suppression block is the
one lever in that research that is wrong for this user, and it is marked so that a later
session reading the guide and not the file does not reach for it.
