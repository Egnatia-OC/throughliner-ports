## After the close

Work that happened after this session's commit, appended rather than folded into the record above.

The close itself broke a rule this project shipped the same day. `done.md`'s Recommend next, rung 2, says to name the next item as information with **no question and no command string ending the message** — the fix that landed as [close-invites-same-session-next] in `340e7ef`. This close ended on a bolded "Start /next when you're ready", and the harness duly offered `/next` as its suggestion chip. That is the precise outcome the rule exists to prevent, produced on the rule's first real test by the project that authored it. Recorded here rather than quietly corrected, because a shipped rule failing immediately is worth more as evidence than as an embarrassment: it is the fourth instance this session of a rule being read, understood, and then not followed by the session that had just read it.

The user then turned the same mechanism into a design. Their observation is that the suggestion chips are useful and should not be disabled — the recommendation is often *only* legible in the chip, which is how they find what Claude is actually proposing — and that the real problem is which command lands there. Their proposal: mid-session asks should not end on the close option, burying it mid-sentence and putting continue last since continue is the commonly taken route; recommendations should surface last so they are clearer and more likely to be picked up; and the close should recommend running /clear or starting a fresh session, leaving the chip generator no /next to grab. The mechanism they are exploiting is already documented in `done.md` — the harness offers the slash command it just saw — but only ever as a hazard. Nothing had noticed it also runs in the useful direction, and nothing had tried to aim it. Filed as [message-ending-drives-the-suggestion-chip], with the honest limit written into the item: shaping the method's prose around an undocumented third-party heuristic is defensible only because two of the three parts are independently right under existing rules and would stand if the chips vanished.

A related check was run and answered: the chips are not the plugin's and nothing in the user's settings controls them. `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_PROMPT_SUGGESTION=false` would disable them wholesale, which is not what is wanted — a blunt off-switch does not distinguish a useful chip from a badly aimed one.
