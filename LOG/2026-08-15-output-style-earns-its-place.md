The style was 616 words, auto-applied at system-prompt priority, and its rules were stated three times over — in the user's personal global instructions, in the method's always-loaded rules, and again here. The user's decision, in her own words at processing: she really thinks it needs to go, and if she did not hear about it for almost two months then it needs to go; anything duplicated in the always-loaded rules should go, and the rest can be incorporated as Claude sees fit.

Five statements were duplicated and died with the file. Three were unique and had to move or be lost: the narration-cadence rule (how often to speak, and the three occasions that warrant it), the written-file length rule, and the counterweight that length is free where it carries substance.

The written-file rule was the contested one, and it landed structurally rather than by careful wording. It contradicts two statements in the same always-loaded file — the Captures authoring standard ("completeness matters more than compression") and the index-line rule ("no length cap of any kind"). Migrating it as a fourth freestanding statement about length would have made that contradiction visible and worse. The user's correction at processing was that the fix must be structural, not a carefully-worded sibling, because the method's own wording rule says to express a qualification as structure and its admission rule says to name the parent first. So the parent was named — the authoring standard itself — and the rule landed as one provision with two scopes: the record is written for completeness, a deliverable written to disk is written to what the task needs. That relationship is what the corpus was missing, and stating it was the actual work. The item's own condition was that the rule not land at all unless it could be worded so it cannot license compressing a queue item or a LOG entry; the condition is met by both scopes sitting in one block.

The cadence rule went in as its own Communication bullet, carrying the distinction that made it worth keeping: how often to speak is a separate question from how long a message is, and in a session full of tool calls it is the one that decides how much the user reads. The counterweight went in as a fifth clause on the existing message-shape bullet, where it reads as a continuation of the parent's opening words and spends no slot.

What is lost is priority, not content. `force-for-plugin: true` meant the harness applied the style to every consumer whatever else they had chosen; the migrated rules now reach a session as ordinary always-loaded steering, and a consumer's own output style is no longer overridden. That is stated in SPEC rather than left to be discovered.
