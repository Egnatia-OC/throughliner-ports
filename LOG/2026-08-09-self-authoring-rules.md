# 72f4fe9 — The authoring heuristic replaced by `self-authoring-rules.md`, an admission-and-eviction gate, with rationale moved out of the operative rule and split by audience

`resources/authoring-heuristic.md` was in force for the entire period plugin-behaviour.md grew from 6,162 to 21,445 words, and it was applied honestly throughout — that is the whole diagnosis. Six of its seven checks either add text to each rule (quantify the target, show an exemplar, state the scope in words, name the pattern with its replacement) or actively resist cutting. Every addition passed its checks and the file grew anyway. More decisively, none of the seven asks whether a rule should exist at all, or what comes out to make room: it is a quality filter applied to text someone has already decided to add.

The replacement charges the real cost in its opening paragraph, because without that each individual addition still looks free. The binding limit is a **count of instructions**, not a word count — models follow roughly 150–200 reliably, and past that adding one causes fewer to be followed. Worse, an inapplicable rule is not filtered out selectively; irrelevant content causes wholesale dismissal, with semantically similar but inapplicable rules as the principal mechanism. A large body of same-sounding behavioural rules is exactly that shape.

Four parts in use order. **Admission** opens with the question that precedes the other four — which existing rule does this amend? — borrowed from legislation's distinction between an amendment and a freestanding provision, which maps onto the ceiling exactly: a freestanding rule consumes one of the slots a model follows reliably, an amendment consumes none. **Eviction** gains two named mechanisms rather than an exhortation: consolidation, where the repeal of the priors is the essential half, and recasting, which is the borrowed answer to the one question the bloat research could not answer, having found essentially nothing on removing existing instructions. **Distribution** carries the already-settled limit that routing works for reference material and fails for standing behavioural rules, since a session cannot fetch a rule it has never read — without which distribution becomes a way of hiding bloat rather than removing it. **Wording** keeps one check, write it as an action, plus the legal drafting devices, all forms of one instruction: express a qualification as structure, not as explanation.

Two things the queue item deliberately left open were settled here. The old "Rules about writing rules" section held a hook-versus-sharper-wording escalation rule, which is admission test 4 in a different form, so it folded in — losing its two worked examples, since per-rule worked examples are the excluded growth engine and are how the predecessor justified its own length. And the "4.8 — the authoring pass" section went entirely: docset A retired, its four model-specific checks were all prescription-*adding*, and a document that reads as live gives live instructions. The honesty note that survived the recovery is kept — there is now no written account of what the current models steer on, and this only stops the document pretending otherwise.

The rationale split is a correction that arrived mid-run, and it is the most important thing recorded here. The /plan session settled that rationale moves out of the operative rule and into the LOG entry that decided it, and that went into the item before this run started. Drafting a public announcement immediately afterwards, the user spotted the flaw: *our session logs aren't accessible in other projects*. Verified — the package ships `docs/`, `docs-b/`, `hooks/`, `skills/`, `templates/`, `scripts/` and `output-styles/`, and ships neither `LOG/` nor `resources/`. So for anyone not developing the method, relocating a rule's rationale to the LOG does not move it, it deletes it, which directly undercuts the throughline the product exists for. The legal source resolves this rather than causing it: explanatory memoranda are *published* alongside the statute, not withheld. The error was reading "reasoning lives elsewhere" as "reasoning lives somewhere only we can see". So the why splits by audience — consumer-facing reasoning to the shipped FAQ, authoring-decision reasoning to the deciding LOG entry, operative rule bare in both cases. The FAQ won over shipping a new explanatory-notes file (a fresh artifact to keep in sync when the FAQ already does the job) and over accepting the loss (cheapest, honest to how law works in practice, but it guts the throughline for exactly the audience the product serves). Because the correction arrived before the build reached this item, no LOG-only wording was ever written — this is a clause included, not a document corrected.

One counter-force bounds the split rather than defeating it: docset B's fidelity audit found rules that had been *stated inside* their why-clauses and were lost when those clauses went, so moving a why must not take an operative statement with it. That warning is in the document.

The document is subject to its own admission test and states its own maximum size at 1,200 words; it landed at 1,159.

No FAQ entry: the gate is host-only, not in the plugin package, and consumers never author method rules. Not done, and tracked by its capture rather than here: the unposted announcement draft still tells readers the reasoning lives in session logs they can read, which is false for every reader who is not developing the method.
