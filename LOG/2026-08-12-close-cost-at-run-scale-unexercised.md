# e5d169b — The short LOG entry becomes the default; full depth is bought at build time

`done-build.md` already asked for depth to be decided per item — full where the reasoning was contested or an alternative seriously weighed, short for mechanical items. On a sixteen-entry close, nearly every entry came out at full depth anyway. The rule asked for a judgment and provided no moment that forces it.

`next-build.md` gains one line: an item wanting a full-depth entry writes a depth line at build time naming which trigger it meets, and an item that writes nothing gets the short form. `done-build.md`'s depth paragraph is rewritten so the short form is the stated default and full depth is what that line buys, rather than a judgment the close makes from scratch over every item.

**The route was the user's, in their own words** — short by default with an explicit opt-in — over the item's own proposal of a depth call recorded for every item. The reasoning for offering that fork is Claude's: a carrier makes the judgment *recorded*, it does not make it *happen*, and the reason sixteen entries came out long is that full depth was the unstated default and writing less reads as withholding. Inverting the default is the lever; the carrier only makes the exception visible.

**It does not cap entry length and must never be built as one.** The existing two triggers are word-for-word unchanged; only which side of them is the default moved. A number here would be the bare-threshold shape the method bans.

**One line in each document was a hard constraint, and it held** — measured, because a fix for close cost that added a section to two of the documents just measured for size would have moved the cost rather than removed it. `done-build.md` went 1,112 → 1,099 words (shorter), `next-build.md` 2,157 → 2,224.

Shipped rather than host-only, since any consumer running a multi-item /next reaches the same close — so it takes an FAQ entry, written for someone who will notice their session records getting shorter and should be able to find out why. That entry says plainly that nothing is capped and that the reasoning on their queue items is unaffected.
