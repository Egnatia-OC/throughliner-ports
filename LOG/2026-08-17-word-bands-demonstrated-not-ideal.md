# dc52025 — the word bands stand, the claim narrows to "demonstrated sufficient", and four consequences follow

Four captures from the measurement run were taken as one subject, governed by a concern the user raised before any of them was opened: that July's figures may have been elevated into THE standard when they are only what we used to have — real evidence that the work can be done at lower counts, and no evidence at all about an ideal length.

That is conceded and written into the rule rather than left in conversation. The bands mark lengths **demonstrated to be sufficient**, not ideal ones. The method's own limit rule already says a stated derivation makes a figure traceable and revisable rather than correct; what was missing was that sentence attached to these bands.

The honest ambiguity is stated too, because it cannot be resolved from the evidence available: a band firing on the typical artifact is equally consistent with a bloated corpus and with a band set too tight, and nothing in the measurement distinguishes them. What pushes against the too-tight reading is the second population rather than the first — 157 entries from the project's earlier life at a median of 183, inside the band. Sufficiency shown twice, never optimality.

The four consequences. The advisory says its piece once per run rather than on every write, which removes the constant firing without weakening the figure. A band applies to an item as it stands rather than to the text being added — demonstrated by this very session, where every processed item grew by an appended paragraph and no single write came near a band. The index line's band gets the cost model it actually rests on: the index is read in full by Claude on every retrieve, so its length is a toll rather than something skimmed past. And the plan-entry split stops being undefined — a planning decision is a disposition on a queue item, which carries a slug, a filename and an index line, so it splits per item processed exactly as a build entry splits per item built.

Rule gate: run — one clause narrowing the claim on the bands shipped the day before, plus three consequences on the same parent; no freestanding rule and no slot spent.
