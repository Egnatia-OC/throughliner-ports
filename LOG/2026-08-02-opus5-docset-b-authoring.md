# [HASH] — Authored docset B in full (11 docs, 39% lighter) and refined its authoring register at the phase-2 checkpoint

Phase 2 of the Opus 5 work. Docset B — the light 5-series docset — now exists in full as a sibling `docs-b/` directory with exact filename parity to docset A, authored by subtraction from the frozen A.

The run was built around the checkpoint the item designed: author one representative section, then judge how a 5-series model actually reads it before committing the whole docset. That checkpoint ran as specified, including its model switch — the sample was authored on Opus 4.8, the session switched to Opus 5, and the read was judged there rather than on a 4.8 read.

**The register was refined at the checkpoint, not adopted as drafted.** The item pre-committed to SaP-style typed pseudocode as the primary register, with PromptMN-style annotated prose named as the fallback if pseudocode read badly. The outcome was neither: a partial go, with the boundary redrawn by *content type* rather than by layer. The settled register is **structure in typed blocks, everything else in prose, tags inline** — a typed block is earned only by content with real structure (a dispatch table, a definition, a set of conditions, a branch).

Three pieces of evidence moved it. First, the fences were carrying the easy content: the two most-slipped rules in the sample section stayed prose in the pseudocode draft too, so pseudocode carried mechanics that would be followed anyway while prose still carried everything that actually slips. Second, prose inside a code fence is a costume — lines like `purpose = the one human checkpoint…` lose wrapping and gain no precision; not a bespoke *syntax*, but a bespoke *semantics*, which is what the Grammar-Prompting caveat is really about. Third and decisively, the format cost correctness: docset A states "don't pick an item past the marker" as a standing rule, and the pseudocode draft demoted it to a comment inside one branch, silently narrowing a global rule. That error was caught and fixed in the re-author, and it is the reason a fidelity audit was filed rather than the docset being trusted as-authored.

**The larger reframe: docset B's lightness comes from subtraction, not from notation.** The same token saving was available in plain short prose at better readability. The per-doc results bear this out — the reduction tracks how much load-bearing-for-4.8 why-clause a doc carried, not its length. plugin-behaviour.md fell 56% and plan.md 41%, both dense with rationale-per-rule; done-audit.md fell 4% and done-build.md 11%, already near-pure routing with nothing to shed. Where there were no why-clauses to subtract, B is the same size as A. Total: 255,885 → 156,964 bytes, −39%.

**Filename parity was a deliberate design call** made during the build rather than asked, under ordering-ownership. Phase 3's session_start then swaps only the directory, every cross-reference resolves unchanged, and falling back to A stays a one-word change.
