# [HASH] — Add a write-time third-party privacy rule and an on-demand scrub sweep, conditioned on recorded repo visibility

This item carried a red flag, and it was **cleared at processing** by informed consent. The consent trail itself — what the user was shown, that deleting text does not remove it from git history, the three options offered, and her choice to scrub forward only while keeping the repo public — was recorded at the /plan close that cleared it. Carried through here as the close's lightweight record: the item shipped with its flag cleared.

The risk was realised, not hypothetical. The repo is public; committed log entries named a real third party in an abuse and evidence-gathering context, publicly readable for roughly six weeks, and a consumer project name shipped inside the plugin package to everyone who installed it. That scrub fixed the instance. This builds the mechanism.

The item's stated crux was real but was not the failure, and the correction shaped the build. It said that Claude narrating "I removed X and Y" can't guarantee the text is clean, because the human approves meaning while a scrub is about tokens. True — but nobody scrubbed badly. The text was written and approved by both parties as unremarkable, because at the time neither was thinking about a third party at all. **The failure was upstream of any approval gate**, which means a gate firing at approval would not reliably have caught it. So the primary defence is a write-time rule that removes the judgment call rather than adding one: third-party names, case details, and the nature of someone else's private matter are never needed for a method lesson, and "a consumer project" carries the same finding.

The sweep is the half the capture omitted entirely, and the half that actually finds things. A rule only protects what comes after it; the existing exposure was six weeks old and was found by a grep in seconds. It runs on demand and has two passes with honestly different guarantees — an exhaustive known-string pass, and a deliberately over-reporting proper-noun pass for a human to read. A sentence-starter filter cut the second pass to 80 candidates on this repo, which is reviewable. Every run prints the history warning unconditionally, because implying that an edit undoes an exposure is the most damaging thing this could do.

Mechanical versus judged was settled by evidence, against the capture's expectation. You cannot enumerate names in advance, but once a name is known a grep finds every instance instantly and completely, which no judgment pass matches for thoroughness. Script for exhaustiveness, stated rule for prevention, and no reliance on a per-approval judgment gate as the primary defence.
