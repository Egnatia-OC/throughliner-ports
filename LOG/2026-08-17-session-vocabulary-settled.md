# [HASH] — a run is a command executing, a session is the chat: five captures collapse into an audit and a corrections build

Five captures from the terminology survey were taken as one subject, because settling them apart would have answered the same question five times. Four of them were waiting on a decision only the user could make.

She chose a third shape over the two the survey had recorded. Not a `<command>` session pattern, and not three named slots with another word for everything else, but a split of the two ideas: a **run** is a command executing — a /plan run, a /next run — and a **session** is the chat. Her reason: "session" is already in widespread use meaning the chat, so this separates the two rather than adding qualifiers to a word doing two jobs. She also corrected Claude's objection that "the close" would compete with a /done run — it is residual language from a step that no longer exists, so it was no argument against anything.

Her second question shaped the work: does this need an audit designed to run immediately before the change is applied wholesale? It does. The existing survey enumerated collocations, not occurrences — a floor on how many meanings exist, never a ceiling — and never reached the FAQ's 185 or SPEC's 88, the consumer-facing texts. Finding the occurrences is a grep; deciding what each one means is judgment on every line, which is this project's own test for when an audit survives. It runs immediately before the corrections rather than now, because the law-prose restyle rewrites the same files in between.

The five captures were deleted once their content was carried across, and the dead slug references in the two new items were repaired by stating the facts directly.
