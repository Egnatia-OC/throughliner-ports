# d632d6a — Corpus-wide audit against two lenses: seventeen findings, all captured, none dropped

The first audit to read the whole docset. The 2026-08-09 compliance audit read only `plugin-behaviour.md` and `CLAUDE.md`; it excluded the skill docs from its instruction *count* on sound grounds — they load only when their skill runs — but its own text claimed they stayed in scope for its lenses, and its files-touched list shows they were never opened. So most of the corpus by volume had been read against no lens at all. This pass opened every procedure doc, the templates, the hooks' user-facing strings and the self-authoring gate.

Two lenses, one pass, because both ask the same question of every rule: was this rule ever adequately specified?

**Lens 1 comes from the user's own principle**, in their words on 2026-08-09: anything described in terms of what not to do only means the rule of what TO do was never adequately described in the first place. Applied across the corpus it separated cleanly into two groups, and the distinction turned out to matter more than the count. Where a positive statement exists nearby, the prohibition is a wording problem — findings 4, 5, 6 and 9 are that shape, and the fix is to lead with the action. Where no positive statement exists anywhere, the rule genuinely was never worked out: `done.md`'s "do not skip the judgment steps" and its "never write a meta-description" are the two clean instances, and both are one sentence away from being complete rules.

Finding 7 is the case that resisted the lens and is recorded because of it. The `[user]` walk-through branch carries five prohibitions around one positive rule, which looks like the worst instance in the corpus — but the positive rule there *is* adequately specified, and the prohibitions are residue from repeated real failures that the lifecycle text openly documents. The lens does not condemn them. What it did surface is that the same set is stated twice, in `next.md` and again in `plugin-behaviour.md`, in prohibition form both times. A rule that already works, charged twice.

Finding 1 is the audit's own honest test, and it passed. `[gate-amendments-subordination-and-placement]` deliberately left `resources/self-authoring-rules.md`'s prohibition-framed §4 heading unfixed so this audit had a chance to catch it unaided. It did, and it also caught three of that section's seven bullets carrying the same defect — which that item does not cover.

**Lens 2 is the subordination test** from `resources/research/legislative-prose-syntax.md`: a unit is genuinely subordinate only when at least two parallel units exist, each reads as a continuation of the parent's opening words, all share one grammatical function, every modifier points only at the opening words or its own unit, and none is a complete sentence. The last is the test the corpus fails most — a complete sentence formatted as a nested bullet is a freestanding rule wearing a bullet, and spends one of the ~150–200 slots a model follows reliably.

Its most valuable result is finding 10: `plugin-behaviour.md`'s Communication section runs eleven complete-sentence bullets, of which at least four share a subject and a grammatical function and would convert. Four slots recovered from the file the last count put at roughly 218 — the largest available conversion in the always-loaded corpus.

The lens also produced a result worth stating against itself. `plan.md`, `setup.md`, `done.md` and `next.md` all carry bullet lists that would convert, but those docs are fetched when their skill runs rather than always-loaded, so the ceiling argument does not apply to them with the force it has for `plugin-behaviour.md`. Findings 11 to 14 are filed with that weakness recorded in each, rather than presented as equivalent to finding 10. What survives for them is legibility and consistency — and, for 12, 13 and 14, a sharper point the lens found incidentally: three procedure docs end in a "Rules" section that restates their own body, which is two copies of a rule that can be edited apart with nothing noticing.

Three findings fell outside both lenses and are the ones with the most immediate consequence. Finding 15: the shipped `CLAUDE-TEMPLATE.md` carries the same duplicated rules that `[evict-claude-md-duplicated-rules]` repeals, so that item as scoped cleans this project and keeps emitting the defect into every consumer project created afterwards. Finding 16: the same template still tells users to record test procedures for "test entries", a flavor `done.md` says plainly is retired. Finding 17: `plan.md` hardcodes the queue mover's path while `done-plan.md` explicitly instructs deriving it from the plugin root and never hardcoding — and `plan.md`'s version resolves only in this project, so the skip-to-defer step would fail in every consumer's.

That third one gained a sibling during the same session. The shell-write guard in `pre_tool_use.py` recommends the same tool at a third path, which resolves nowhere; a blocked session followed it, found nothing, and filed a capture concluding the tool had never existed. That capture had to be rewritten minutes later. Three documents, one tool, three different paths — and the failure is not that no help was given but that authoritative-sounding help sent the session away from the working tool.

All seventeen were approved as one set with no contest, which is the bulk-approval inversion working as designed: a deterministic result set produced by criteria the user approved when they queued the audit.
