# d82f538 — The rule gate is narrated where it fires, so a decision is visible while it can still be objected to

Claude proposes the rule, Claude runs the gate, Claude writes the disposition recording that it was admitted. `CLAUDE.md` already conceded that neither board check reading those lines can tell an honest disposition from a dishonest one. What this adds is that the one party with an independent view had never seen a gate decision at the moment it was made.

**The item's own framing needed correcting first.** It said the user is *structurally outside the loop*. That was true when the gate sat at the build or the close; it stopped being true when the gate moved to /plan's keep-step, which is a conversation with her in it. What is missing is not her presence but any moment that puts the decision in front of her — the gate runs silently, writes its verdict onto the item, and she finds out later only by going looking with the dispositions listing.

So the build is narration, not an approval turn. When the gate fires at the keep-step, the item's message says what rule is proposed, which parent it amends, and what the gate decided — one or two sentences folded into a message she is already reading, never a turn of its own. That clears the item's own bar: it introduces a **party**, not a field. The item explicitly barred resolving this with another artifact, on the ground that a second required line written by the same author creates no independence.

**The limit is stated wherever this is described, and must not be softened.** A narrated gate is Claude's decision announced, not the user's decision made. It gives her a chance to object where she currently gets none. It gives her no veto and makes no dishonest disposition detectable.

The approver framing came from the words that raised this, in the user's own question of 2026-08-13 — *of what relevance is approval when you have literally never been asked* — and the corpus never asserted it. There was nothing to correct, so that half has no build.
