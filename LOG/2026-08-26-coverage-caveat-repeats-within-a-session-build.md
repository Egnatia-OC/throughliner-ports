# 2c76e53 — Coverage caveat: full sentence once per session, a one-clause back-reference after

The line about not being able to tell whether earlier conversation has dropped out
of view appeared three times across one pair of sessions — twice inside a single
session, minutes apart. Every instance was honest and the rule requiring it was
doing its job, which is why the fix is not to say it less truthfully.

What was settled at planning is that the teaching moment is the first statement.
A non-coder meeting an unfamiliar limitation learns it once; the third telling
reads as boilerplate, and boilerplate is what people learn to skip past — which is
how a genuinely important caveat stops being read at all. That is the cry-wolf
shape this project has repealed measures for twice, which is why something this
small was filed rather than shrugged off.

So the requirement is now two-tier at both specimen sites: the full sentence the
first time a session says it, and after that a one-clause back-reference that
restates the operative fact — that it reaches only what was still in view — without
the full lesson. Held in the conversation with nothing stored, the same shape as
the once-per-rest ask.
