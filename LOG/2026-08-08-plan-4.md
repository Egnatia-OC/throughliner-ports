# [HASH] — Sixteen items processed on the fresh branch: the compression gap named and made buildable, the docset-A retirement's remaining leftovers cleared, and the cycle's audit shape settled

The first planning session on `overnight-blitz-2026-08-09`, working the queue that
the just-merged span left behind. Fifteen unprocessed items plus one the user filed
mid-session; thirteen kept, one skipped, three resolved-and-deleted, and three
folded into other items.

**The session's largest outcome was not on the list when it started.** Asked what
the differential audit had yielded, the user said what they had originally wanted
from it: *"something that would audit the whole docset and help us find all the
bloat and shrink it"*, and diagnosed the underlying condition — *"the docs just
keep growing because instead of actually editing the method, Claude is just adding
and adding and adding."* Checking the artifacts confirmed the confusion was caused
by them rather than by misremembering: two different jobs share the word "audit"
and **neither shrinks anything**. `resources/consistency-audit-plan.md` carries a
differential mode and a full-corpus mode, and both ask whether the documents agree
with each other and with the code. The full run of 2026-08-07 is even filed as
`2026-08-07-pre-compression-doc-audit.md` — it is the safety check you run *before*
compressing, and the compression it was named for has never had a plan, a queue
item, or a phase in the cycle. That became [compression-pass-plan-and-cycle-audit-roles],
now cleared, with the four things its plan must state settled here so the build is
authoring rather than design — including the boundary that matters most: the
why-clause that makes a rule followed is **not** bloat, and the pass must not treat
it as fat.

**That concern then shaped the rest of the session, which is why it is recorded as
a thread rather than one item.** [authoring-heuristic-has-no-live-model-pass]
arrived asking for a new 5-series authoring checklist to replace the retired 4.8
one; reading the doc reversed it. The 4.8 section is **not** marked historical
anywhere in the file itself — only in CLAUDE.md — so the artifact consulted at
authoring time reads as fully live and would hand anyone a checklist tuned to a
dropped model. All seven of its checks were read and they split cleanly: three are
model-agnostic (lead with the decision, show the shape with an exemplar, guard
against over-terseness) and four add prescription, which is what the current models
want less of. So the build is now a deletion plus a promotion rather than a new
section — one shorter document that is true, instead of a longer one carrying a
tombstone. The same reasoning shortened [self-built-fixtures-assert-the-assumption]
to three or four sentences with no incident retelling, and moved its home from the
authoring heuristic to CLAUDE.md, because the rule fires when someone is writing a
test rather than when they are authoring method text.

**Four docset-A retirement leftovers were cleared in-session rather than queued.**
[fences-item-ripple-list-outdated-by-docset-a-retirement] asked for one item's
ripple list to be re-cut; grepping found the damage was wider — **three** items
still named deleted docset-A files in their build lists. All three were repaired
here and the item deleted, on the reasoning that editing work-item text *is*
planning work and queueing it would send it to a session obliged to hand it back.
One survivor was left alone deliberately: a line recording what shipped at
`ea272f6` names both docsets as history, not as somewhere to edit.

**Three design calls were settled rather than deferred.** The audit finding
[correction-disarm-vs-next-halt-contradiction] was framed as either/or — the
correction-disarms rule wins, or /next's halt does. Neither does: a build session
may **append one dated line** to a corrected item ("correction filed, see [slug]")
and nothing more, which satisfies the disarm rule's purpose without letting an
unattended run edit item prose or move the readiness boundary. The decision was
made on this session's own evidence in both directions — the fences ripple item was
the not-amending failure recurring *after* the disarm rule shipped, and the
authority-expansion risk is the one [concurrent-session-support] already warns
about. [merge-cycle-as-queue-machinery]'s three open questions all resolved cheaply
(seeded at soak-end step 6; the blitz item is an ordinary build below the line, not
`[user]`, per the over-tag guard; a documented step rather than a hook). And
[learning-mode-fork-at-setup] was sharpened and skipped, with its output-style
question closed by elimination: only one output style is active at a time and this
plugin ships its own, so learning mode must be in-method guidance rather than a
style switch.

**A concurrency incident happened mid-session and is recorded because the safety
held.** A second session was open on this folder and rewrote the advisory at the
top of Unprocessed after this session had read the queue. The next edit here raised
the "file modified on disk" warning; work stopped, `git status` and a diff
established what had changed, both writes turned out to have landed in different
regions, and nothing was damaged. The user closed the other session and work
resumed. This is the same failure condition that in August destroyed an item's
heading and reached a commit — with the warning dismissed twice as the queue
mover's doing. **This is the first recorded time that rule held**, and it is
captured as evidence for [concurrent-session-support], along with the observation
that the warning detects *collisions*, not *presence*: a session that only read
would never have known.

everything cleared grows the soak-end audit's span, and that rule-changing work is
the most expensive kind for it to cover, and chose to keep clearing. Recorded as a
trade taken deliberately rather than a warning to re-raise.
