# 7e3c1c8 — the word bands say what they are evidence of, and the advisory fires once per run

The bands now state that they mark lengths **demonstrated to be sufficient**, never ideal ones, and the advisory says its piece once per run rather than on every write.

The user's concern is the governing one and it is conceded in the text rather than in conversation: the July figures may have been elevated into *the* standard when they are only what we used to have — real evidence the work can be done at lower counts, and no evidence at all about what an ideal length is. The method's own limit rule already says a stated derivation makes a figure traceable and revisable rather than correct; what was missing was that sentence attached to these bands, so it does not have to be re-argued.

The measurement that prompted it: medians against ceilings ran 329 against 265 for captures, 412 against 345 for work items, 400 against 345 for build entries, 62 against 60 for index lines. Only plan entries sat under. So the breach action fired on the typical case rather than an outlier — the cry-wolf shape, arriving from the figures rather than the wording.

The honest ambiguity is written in because it cannot be resolved from the firing rate: a band firing on the typical artifact fits a bloated corpus and a too-tight band equally. What pushes against the second is a *different* population — 157 entries from the project's earlier life, median 183, inside the build-entry band. Two independent samples agreeing is sufficiency shown twice, never optimality.
