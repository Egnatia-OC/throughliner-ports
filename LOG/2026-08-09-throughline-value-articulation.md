# [HASH] — Two FAQ entries written on what the recorded reasoning is for and what approving it buys, name-neutrally so the rename stays free to introduce its own term

The user raised this on 2026-07-30 while drafting a Discord help article, and the problem they named has not changed: the existing framing — that you can sometimes ask why something is the case — reads as a minor lookup feature, and nobody accepts the cost of approving drafts and writing session records for a lookup feature. The value was being undersold by its own documentation.

The headline the entries lead with is that the value is not occasional why-lookup at all. It is that the user's intent keeps running the project after Claude has forgotten everything. Claude's memory resets every session; the recorded reasoning is why a fresh, short, even cheaper-model session still builds the project the way the user meant, instead of re-deriving intent from code and guessing wrong. That is the product rather than a side feature, and stating it that way is the whole point of the rewrite.

The user's branches framing carried into the text: it is not one file. Intent lives in SPEC, the reasoning rides every queue item, history lives in LOG — and because SPEC and QUEUE are read *during* planning and building, the reasoning shapes every plan and build silently rather than only answering a question someone thought to ask. The second entry takes the overhead objection head-on rather than leaving it implied: approving each draft is what makes the record genuinely the user's and therefore trustworthy, since a reason you never vouched for is one you cannot trust on read-back; the writing pays a small cost now against the much larger archaeology cost later; and externalised intent is exactly what makes short, cheap sessions work at all.

The sequencing constraint decided the shape more than anything else. The text is written name-neutrally — "the record of why," "the reasoning behind each decision" — because the concept name arrives only with `[rename-to-throughliner]`, which is below the line and unscheduled, and whose build traces its scope by grepping literal identity strings. Branding the concept here would either pre-empt that rename or entrench a term it would then have to sweep out. This was verified rather than assumed: neither concept name appears anywhere in the shipped FAQ today, so name-neutral text matches what consumers already read and is not a compromise.

The destination was settled this session on Claude's recommendation and the user's agreement: the explanation goes to the FAQ now, and the SPEC and README positioning stays folded into `[rename-to-throughliner]`, which rewrites positioning wholesale and will lead with this framing. Splitting by artifact is what lets the explanation ship immediately without waiting on an unscheduled identity change. The authoring gate does not bind here — this adds no instruction, it is user-facing explanation fetched on demand, so it costs nothing against the instruction ceiling, and under the distribution rule the FAQ is precisely where it belongs rather than in always-loaded prose.

The existing entry on where to *find* design rationale was left in place as the mechanical answer and cross-linked to the new one, rather than being rewritten to carry both jobs.
