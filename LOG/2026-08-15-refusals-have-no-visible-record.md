# 0e62afe — Refusals get a scannable record, surfaced where the user actually looks

The finding is structural rather than an incident. A kept item sits in the queue where the user sees it. A built item leaves a vanished queue entry and a LOG entry named after its slug. A **refused** proposal leaves one sentence inside one LOG entry and nothing anywhere else — no queue line, no index signal, no board entry. So "what did I ask for that did not get built?" was answerable only by reading every entry, which is exactly the cost the user objected to. And `CLAUDE.md` rests the gate's entire value on its power to refuse: a /plan-sited gate can refuse, which a build-sited or close-sited one cannot, "and that is the whole gain". The output the design is proudest of was the one output nothing surfaced.

Checked before building, because the item must not rest on a premise the record contradicts: nothing slipped through silently in the prompting instance. `b4de5bf`'s LOG entry records that a proposal was tested against the gate, failed it, and that the user chose the narrow fix after being shown the result. The refusal happened with her present — which is the entire reason the gate is sited at /plan.

Three answers, settled at processing, and the third was corrected by the user mid-discussion. It is a **mode behind a flag, not a fifth check**, because nothing is wrong when it prints — `rule_signals.py` keeps two classes apart and counts only the four that can fire, so a listing joins neither. It reads **every** disposition rather than only refusals, since the user's question is what was asked for and not built, and the same parse yields the rest for one more column. And it is **surfaced at a /plan opening, windowed to entries since the last planning session**. Claude first recommended on-demand only; she refused it with the question that settles it — **"how would I even know to demand it?"** — which is the siteless-check failure this project has recorded five times, walked into by the recommendation itself. Claude's counter-proposal of a five-entry window was then tightened by her to *since the last plan session*, which tracks the moment she actually looks rather than an arbitrary count.

Windowed rather than complete because a report listing every refusal ever made grows without bound, and an ever-growing line is one a reader learns to skip. Windowed, it goes quiet by itself when nothing has been refused.

**The guard is printed wherever the listing prints.** It reports what a disposition *claims*. It cannot say whether a refusal was correct, and it cannot see a proposal dropped in conversation before any disposition was written.

It must not go in `plan.md`, and that was nearly the build's mistake: `plan.md` ships, `rule_signals.py` does not, and consumers never author method rules — a step there would be a check that can never fire in any consumer project.
