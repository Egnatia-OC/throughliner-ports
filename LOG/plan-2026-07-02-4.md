# [HASH] — Queue redesign founding decision: two-section work-line model + first three batches queued

This session opened the queue-redesign branch's founding work. The decision, user-vouched: replace the old work-type model (build/test/audit batches, parking, dependencies, red flags, deferred tests) with a single notion of "work" carrying two properties — who does it (default Claude; user-work marked `[user]`) and whether it's processed (which of two sections it sits in). QUEUE.md collapses to two headings, Processed and Unprocessed. A capture becomes unprocessed work; the old Captures section dissolves into Unprocessed. Parking and blocking are abolished — proven unreliable to unpark — so work is either in the queue or deleted. Each line keeps a lean rationale, a lightweight slug (LOG traceability only), and a persistent provenance label ("captured by you / by Claude"). One stop device: the existing cleared-to-run line. /next builds Claude-work top-down and hands over at the first user-work line.

Decided in discussion, preserved: red flags and deferred tests are both dropped for now, not relocated; the slug survives only because it links a LOG entry back to the queue line (its dependency-header and cross-reference jobs die with parking/blocking); the two "who"s are kept distinct — who does the work vs who captured it. On the overwhelming parked backlog, the user chose a clean break: rather than triage ~20 parked items, start a fresh queue and leave the rest in git history. Claude was delegated the pick of what to carry forward — three items, chosen because none is obsoleted by the redesign; the big clusters ([cruise-control], [test-concept-redesign], the batch-type items) are left in history because the redesign absorbs them.
