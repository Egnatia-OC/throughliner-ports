# dc52025 — no dependency field for captures: a known ordering is written on both items instead

Raised by the user on noticing that a dependency Claude had just written into a capture's prose was one that should have been a field. `Blocked by:` is defined for the held region below the readiness line; a capture in Unprocessed has nowhere to put one, so ordering there can only be a sentence. Two instances in one session, from opposite directions: her instruction that one item wait on another was prose and was processed against, and Claude's note that one capture waited on the word-band work was prose and was honoured only by chance.

Claude proposed a `Process after:` field. She defeated it, and the reframing is what settles the item. The dependency is not "process this after that" — it is that the work a capture may produce could block work described anywhere else, and **not every unprocessed item describes work at all; the work is often inferred and only becomes definite at processing.** So a field filled in at filing would ask Claude to name a fix before anyone has designed one, using space meant for describing the problem, and might record no input of hers at all.

The general case therefore has no field-shaped answer and does not need one: an undiscoverable dependency becomes visible when the item is processed, which is the moment the whole-queue read settled the same day now covers.

What remains is the narrow case where an ordering is already known, which both of today's instances were. The method already has the device — a relationship written as a slug in the prose of *both* items — and the standing rule already says a relationship not written as a slug does not exist. What failed was writing it on one side only.

Permitting `Blocked by:` in Unprocessed was refused too: it means "cannot be built until X ships" and would make the lint, the digest and the dependency facts wrong about a section they do not describe.

Rule gate: run — a clause on the existing slug-reference rule; no freestanding rule, no format change, no ripple.
