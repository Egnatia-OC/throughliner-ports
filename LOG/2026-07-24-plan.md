# d8a1cf1 — Merged three /plan-flow captures into one cleared build [plan-processing-flow-redesign], and reordered Unprocessed to lead with the `[user]`-line lifecycle cluster

This session was scoped by the user to one thing: settle the cluster of captures about how /plan processes its queue, now that /next is the single queue-clearing runner. Four captures sat adjacent and all touched the same part of plan.md's processing flow, so designing them separately would have meant four overlapping edits to one step.

The first item, [plan-serves-single-runner], was deleted rather than kept. Its own text said it was "not a discrete build on its own" — an orientation, not work — and a lens fails the keep test because there is nothing to build. Its orientation (design /plan around serving the single runner) was applied to the cluster instead of being stored as a queue line.

[quickplan-skill] was then kept as the umbrella for a single build rather than as a standalone item, and [skip-to-defer-in-plan-loop] and [capture-then-offer-process-now] were folded into it and deleted as separate lines. The resulting build has four components: a purely-subtractive triage bulk-drop pass at the top of processing; a start-of-processing reorder plus a narrated minimum-unblocker floor; skip-to-defer folded into the checkpoint's existing off-ramps; and a process-now branch replacing the bare "anything else?" after a user files a capture.

Two design points were settled inside that. The droppability test — an item is bulk-droppable only if there is a one-sentence, uncontestable reason it is not worth doing, otherwise it goes to the deep pass — exists to give the pass a hard ceiling, since the pass can only ever delete and never advance work into Processed. And skip-to-defer was deliberately folded into an ask that already exists rather than given its own prompt, because a standalone skip-gate would have re-created the over-asking bug [plan-repeated-progress-approval] is about.

The alternative that lost, and the one most likely to be re-proposed: folding session-sizing ([session-sizing-and-break-lines]) into the same build. It is the intuitive move, because both it and the umbrella's narration appear to answer "how much should this session do." They do not. The narration is a planning-throughput count; session-sizing is a context-budget count. More decisively, the model→build-count advice is toothless without the session-break-line mechanism that the same item still leaves undesigned, and the one way to act on it without that mechanism — placing the cleared-to-run line lower — would re-conflate "safe by dependency" with "safe by context budget," which is the exact conflation the session-break line exists to prevent. So it was carved out and left in Unprocessed intact.

At close, Unprocessed was reordered to lead with the three `[user]`-line lifecycle items, because Processed is currently holding four `[user]` handover lines and the method has no defined way to complete or close any of them — the queue cannot cleanly retire its own work. A full re-sort of all 29 remaining items was considered and not done: moving a line means retyping its full prose block, where a silent transcription slip would corrupt a work line, and nearly all the reorder's value sits at the top. That limitation was itself captured as [reorder-has-no-move-primitive].
