# cb50e2b — a consumer project sent evidence against the build view before it was built, and two of its four limbs land

Mail from SimplySew, running 1.20.0-test12, sent deliberately ahead of the change as evidence of what it might cost. The user is the same person in both projects; the concern is hers, the worked example is that project's.

Their situation: a run had finished six build items and moved into a `[user]` walk-through. Instead of answering step 1 the user went sideways into a long aside about equipment. The run filed two captures out of it, split because they had different dispositions, answered the question she had actually asked, and returned to step 1. Their claim is that none of what made those captures good came from the build.

Four limbs were checked against what this session actually designed rather than accepted as sent, and two of them land. Knowing the captures were not already filed required reading the whole queue — the build view removes that. Recognising which detail was consequential required knowing what a queued item depends on, which is queue knowledge and also removed. But knowing the spec was silent on a product area, so a stated wish could be recorded without becoming a scope claim, required SPEC — and /next reads SPEC at run start, which the build view does not change. And splitting one aside into two items required knowing how items get processed, which is procedure knowledge, loaded either way. **So the concern is real but narrower than sent: it is queue-wide knowledge specifically, not context in general.**

Their sharpest point survives the narrowing. The aside happened during a `[user]` walk-through — the phase where the user is doing the work and talking freely, and therefore where unprompted material arrives most. Context narrowing applied uniformly across a run bites hardest exactly where the raw material is richest.

What partly answers it does not fully. [concurrent-plan-and-build-sessions] puts a planning session alongside the run holding the whole queue, so a capture has somewhere well-informed to go — but the session *hearing* the aside is the build session, the one without queue context, so either the user repeats herself in the other window or something carries it across. That friction lands at the richest moment.

Three options are named and none designed: give the build view the queue's headings and slugs only, which is cheap and restores duplicate-detection without restoring the history; route walk-throughs to the planning session, which reaches the phase they identify and nothing else; or accept the cost and record it. The first was written onto the build-view item as a candidate belonging there rather than in a later item, and the item now carries a read-this-first pointer so a build cannot reach it without meeting the objection.

Their own limit is worth preserving: this shows one good capture drawing on queue-wide context. It does not show what the change actually restricts, since they cannot see it.

**Queue changes:** [build-view-narrows-capture-context] filed to Unprocessed; a pointer to it written onto [split-the-cleared-region-for-concurrent-sessions]. Message archived to `INBOX/archive/`.

**Work processed:** none — routed as a capture, to be weighed at a later planning session on the user's decision to close instead.
