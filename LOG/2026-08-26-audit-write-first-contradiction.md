# 3b094b5 — Write-first's justification is rebuilt on cost and in-chat consent, and the candidate-set exception is admitted

A compliance-audit finding: done.md's wind-down and rescan.md both said to show a candidate set as one numbered message before writing, while their parent — the always-loaded write-first test — listed captures as write-then-report with no carve-out. A close reads both and got opposite instructions.

The parent moved, and further than the finding asked. The user's correction mid-processing is why: write-first rested on the premise that she reads text directly from QUEUE.md, a position she has walked back — so the rule's approval story fell, not merely its edge case. What survives is the cost argument, which is her own earlier capture: text shown and then written is produced twice. The replacement approval story is the one shipped alongside this in [keep-approval-reading-burden] — consent happens in conversation, in plain words, before the write, and the file is the record of what was agreed rather than where agreement happens.

The build reworded the justification to carry both halves, added one exception line for a close-time or /rescan candidate set, and reworded the orientation block's second point, which had claimed that reading and approving the record is the product. It now says approving happens in conversation and the record is what that conversation produces — her confirmation of the reworded premise: "that's true to my experience". done.md and rescan.md were left exactly as they were, as the item directed.

Refused at planning: reverting the children to write-first, since the numbered set serves the non-reading user; and repealing write-first wholesale, since the cost argument stands on its own without the reading premise.
