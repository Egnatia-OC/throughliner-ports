# 7e3c1c8 — the vocabulary test now asks whether a term is used in passing or explained

The shipped rule asked whether a term named something in this user's world that you could show them, and had two answers: say it, or don't. There was no room for a term that names nothing showable and is still worth one explanation — and the alternative to that explanation is a longer sentence in its place, every time, forever.

The test is rekeyed on the user's own correction: the ban is not on the term, it is on **unexplained passing use**. Where the user is asking about the procedure itself, Claude names its parts and teaches each term once. Where a term is dropped in passing, it is still translated or omitted, and the list of typical offenders stands.

The "Show it, don't define it" prescription is repealed with its evidence clause. That clause claimed something had been explained repeatedly until somebody opened a file and pointed — and the user, present for the moment it described, said that was simply when she relented. A coincidence had been recorded as a cause and had stood as justification since. What replaces it is one line saying how to explain is Claude's judgment: answer the question actually asked, in the form that answers it.

Rule gate: run — an amendment to the existing vocabulary rule; no freestanding rule and no slot spent. The eviction is the show-first form and its evidence clause, repealed outright. Failure evidence is two recorded instances.
