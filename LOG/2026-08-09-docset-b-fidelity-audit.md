A recovery, not an audit. The audit this item originally asked for already ran, at `b931278` on the evening of 2026-08-02 — hours after the commit the emergency revert restored to — reading all eleven doc pairs against three criteria and producing twelve findings, all approved as drafted. The repairs landed the next day at `f37e332`. The revert discarded both, leaving docset B in the working tree as the pre-audit, pre-repair version carrying all twelve defects.

Re-running it blind would have meant reading 22 documents again and would likely have surfaced a different subset — a fresh pass is *weaker* evidence than the recorded findings, not stronger. So the findings came from the audit's own LOG entry at `b931278` and the fixes from the `docs-b/` diff at `f37e332`, with the recorded finding as the specification wherever a repair no longer applied cleanly. In the event every repair applied cleanly and none needed re-authoring.

The two most consequential were live bugs rather than tidiness. Docset B nowhere defined what makes an editor "recorded" — docset A states it twice, as any value other than the literal `not recorded` — while docset B's own setup still writes that exact literal when the user skips the question. So a B session read a skipped field as a recorded editor and sent a pointer to a file the user had no way to open, and because it pointed, pasted nothing in chat: the user got nothing at all. That definition now lives in one place, with next.md and plan.md pointing at it rather than judging by eye. And the capture line-format block — the one shape all three hooks parse — was malformed, positioning the user-credit and commit stamp in a layout the prose never states; those are now named as prose conventions and taken out of the parsed block.

The third was a class rather than a single loss: docset A carries 27 explicit scope clauses and B carries two. Seven reach-carrying clauses came back as inline prose (six restored, one already present), and the twenty-clause drop stands as settled rather than as debt.

**Two things `f37e332` also shipped were deliberately not restored**, both settled at the 2026-08-09 /plan and both verified absent by grep at this close. A CLAUDE.md rule declaring docset B's monolithic plugin-behaviour.md deliberate — the decision stands, but the standing rule was rejected because the question recurs from the *user* rather than from Claude, so a rule aimed at Claude targets the wrong party; that reasoning lives in `LOG/2026-08-03-docset-b-progressive-disclosure.md`. And a "resuming from a mid-run pause" step in next.md — rejected as misconceived: Claude cannot detect a pause at all, being frozen between turns, so the step attaches to a moment that does not exist, and the bundling it targets is general rather than checkpoint-shaped.

One divergence was preserved rather than "fixed": docset B corrects a wrong within-doc cross-reference that docset A gets wrong, pointing at sub-step 2 where A points at sub-step 3. Recorded again here so a later reader does not undo it as drift.

The audit's authoring lesson is the durable part, and it is not repeated in the procedure docs — it now lives in `resources/self-authoring-rules.md`, written in the same run. Subtraction did not lose the why-clauses it meant to shed. It lost rules that had been *stated inside* those clauses: an exception at the end of a sentence, a definition in a parenthetical, a mechanism named in a subordinate clause. The risk in a subtraction pass is not what a paragraph argues, it is what a paragraph quietly defines while arguing.

No FAQ entry and no SPEC change: these are fidelity repairs to procedure docs, restoring reach and definitions the subtraction dropped. No user-facing feature was added or removed, and no SPEC sentence went wrong or incomplete.
