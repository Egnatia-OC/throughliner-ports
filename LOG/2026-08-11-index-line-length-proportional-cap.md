# [HASH] — A LOG index line is bounded at 20% of the entry it points to [index-line-length-proportional-cap]

The user's instruction, that the index-line guideline gain a proportional length bound — explicitly as a guideline addition, not as extra rationale, which the same guideline forbids.

**The number is measured, not chosen**, which is what makes it the worked example of [derivation-required-for-limits] shipped alongside it. Three entries against their index lines on 2026-08-10: 1,738 words against 231 (13%), 1,055 against 176 (17%), 968 against 184 (19%). The user's first figure was 50%, withdrawn on Claude's showing that it would licence index lines to roughly triple — a growth permit rather than a bound. 20% freezes current practice: the worst current case passes unaltered and nothing may expand past where it already sits.

**Why a proportion rather than a word count.** The entry it summarises is the thing that varies, so a proportion stays correct as entries change length where an absolute number would be the same undeclared guess the repealed 1,200-word ceiling was.

**What it deliberately does not touch.** The content requirement for an index line — the artifact touched, the nature of the change, the filename — is unchanged, and the shipped statement that an entry too short to support the open/skip decision fails even at one line stays. This adds a ceiling, not a floor, and does not reopen the no-length-cap decision by the back door: the cap is relative to the entry, so a long entry may still carry a long line.

The pattern the three-entry sample could not show: violations are overwhelmingly SHORT entries carrying ordinary-length lines, and all three sampled entries were long (968, 1,055, 1,738 words). Every one of the 21 entries written in this run came in under the cap, most at 13–18%, so it binds new work comfortably — but a limit whose own corpus breaks it 87 times is close to the failure [derivation-required-for-limits] exists to prevent.
