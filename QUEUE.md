#### Replace the authoring heuristic with `self-authoring-rules.md` — an admission-and-eviction gate for the method's own rules, pointed at from CLAUDE.md every session [self-authoring-rules]
Captured by you, 2026-08-09, immediately after the emergency revert. Filed after `19ff11b`.
**Why the existing document is the wrong tool.** `resources/authoring-heuristic.md` was in force for the entire period plugin-behaviour.md grew from 6,162 to 21,445 words. It was applied, not skipped. Six of its seven checks either add text to each rule (quantify the target, show an exemplar, state the scope in words, name the pattern with its replacement) or actively resist cutting (guard against over-terseness), so every addition passed its checks honestly and the file grew anyway. More decisively, none of the seven asks whether a rule should exist at all, or what comes out to make room. It is a quality filter with no admission control and no eviction policy, applied to text someone has already decided to add.
**The research that reframes it** (`resources/research/instruction-file-bloat-and-subtraction.md`, 2026-08-09): the binding limit is a *count of instructions*, not a word count — models follow roughly 150–200 reliably, and past that adding one causes fewer to be followed. Irrelevant content causes wholesale dismissal of all instructions rather than selective filtering, so a rule that merely doesn't apply this session is degrading the ones that do. Context-rot research names distractor interference from semantically similar but irrelevant content as a principal mechanism, which is exactly what a large set of same-sounding behavioural rules is. Repository analysis found negative correlation with performance past 1,000 words. Anthropic's guidance covers what to include when authoring; it says essentially nothing about removing what already exists, so the eviction half has to be invented here rather than borrowed.
**Build it in four parts, in use order.**
1. **Admission — does this rule get to exist?** Has this actually failed, more than once, in a way you can point to? Does Claude already do it unprompted? Does it apply to every session or only some? Could a hook do it instead, at no attention cost? A speculative rule fails at the first question.
2. **Eviction — what comes out.** Adding a rule names what it replaces or supersedes: rules mostly arrive as refinements of existing ones, and the superseded version is what never gets removed. Plus a staleness test — is this still true — because a confidently wrong rule is worse than a missing one.
3. **Distribution — where it lives.** Always-loaded versus fetched-on-demand, carrying the limit already settled in `LOG/2026-08-03-docset-b-progressive-disclosure.md`: routing works for reference material and fails for standing behavioural rules, since a session cannot fetch a rule it has never read. Without that constraint, distribution becomes a way of hiding bloat rather than removing it.
4. **Wording — one check.** Write it as an action, not a prohibition. The user's call, 2026-08-09: this is the sole survivor of the old seven; the other six are cut.
**Borrow the structure from legal drafting** (`resources/research/legal-drafting-for-tight-rules.md`, 2026-08-09).
- **Admission gains a question that precedes the other four: which existing rule does this amend?** Legislation distinguishes an amendment from a *freestanding* provision — "a provision of an act that is not an amendment to or repeal of existing law." That is the choice made at every addition here, and freestanding has been the silent default. It maps exactly onto the instruction-count ceiling: a freestanding rule consumes one of the ~150–200 slots a model follows reliably; an amendment consumes none, because it changes a rule already occupying its slot. A change that cannot name a parent rule is either genuinely new territory or — far more often — a refinement whose parent was never looked for.
- **Eviction gains two named mechanisms rather than an exhortation.** *Consolidation*: combine the rules on a topic into one and **repeal the priors** — the repeal is the essential half, and a "clearer restatement" that leaves the old statement standing has doubled the text rather than merged it. *Recasting* (EU law): where amendments have accreted past legibility, repeal and replace with a single new text incorporating the original and all its amendments, substantive changes allowed. Recasting is the borrowed answer to the one question the instruction-bloat research could not answer — it found essentially nothing on removing existing instructions.
- **The wording check gains the drafting devices, all forms of one instruction: express a qualification as structure, not as explanation.** Avoid provisos — a rule followed by a swelling "provided that" qualification is precisely the habit this exists to break. Signal an exception *before* the general rule, so it is never applied and then withdrawn. Use `subject to <X>` as a cross-reference rather than restating the exception, since a restatement costs a paragraph and creates a second copy that drifts. Put multiple exceptions in their own subsection, referenced from the rule. Use short connectives — but, except that, unless, so long as — not explanations. One idea per provision. Don't mix conditions and exceptions in one sentence. Don't hide exceptions: clarity and brevity are both served by structure over prose.

**Rationale moves out of the operative rule — already settled by existing research, never applied (user's call, 2026-08-09).** Legislation states the binding rule bare and puts the reasoning in recitals and explanatory memoranda, which aid interpretation but are not the law. The method does the opposite, and that habit is the single largest contributor to per-rule weight. It is not an open question: `resources/research/opus-5-instruction-compliance.md` records that "the current docset's defining feature (why-clauses travelling with every rule, explicit scope guards) is exactly the over-prescription the 5-series guidance says degrades output," and `fable-5-instruction-compatibility.md` resolves it precisely — "'Carry the why with the rule' — partially obsolete as a compliance technique (Fable complies without the reason)... Keep the why-pipeline; stop treating why-attachment as a compliance requirement." Two independent lines reaching the same split. So: the operative statement stays bare and precise; the reasoning goes to the LOG entry that decided it, which is the non-operative record this project already owns. The narrow exception, by exception and not by default: a rule whose reason is genuinely needed to apply it correctly at its edges keeps one short clause. The counter-evidence is real and bounds the exception rather than defeating it — docset B's fidelity audit found rules that had been *stated inside* their why-clauses and were lost when those clauses went, so moving a why must not take an operative statement with it.

**Two structural requirements.** The document is subject to its own admission test and states its own maximum size — otherwise it becomes what it exists to prevent, which is what happened to its predecessor. And it opens with one paragraph on why additions are not free, carrying the compliance-ceiling and wholesale-dismissal findings: without that, each individual addition still looks costless, which is precisely how the old document was honestly applied all the way to 21,445 words.
**The ceiling stays qualitative** — the user's call, 2026-08-09. No numeric rule budget.
**Explicitly not included:** per-rule worked examples. That is the growth engine, and it is how the current file justifies its own length.
**Two things to resolve during the build, not now.** The old file's "Rules about writing rules" section holds a hook-versus-sharper-wording escalation rule — that is admission test 4 in a different form, so fold it in rather than carrying it separately. And the "4.8 — the authoring pass" section is calibrated for the frozen fallback docset and for the model appetite that caused the problem; with six of its checks cut it has little left, so decide whether anything survives or the section goes entirely.
**Start from the recovered text, don't write from scratch (found 2026-08-09).** `resources/self-authoring-rules.md` already exists at `pre-revert-2026-08-09`, under the same name chosen here independently. 761 words, already renamed from the heuristic, with the 4.8 pass already deleted — recover its reasoning for that deletion, which is sound and still applies: docset A retired 2026-08-08 so 4.8 is no longer a supported model, and its four model-specific checks were all prescription-*adding*, the opposite of what the 5-series wants. It also keeps a useful honesty note, that there is now no written account of what the current models steer on, and that this only stops the document pretending otherwise. **But it does not make the fix designed here** — it has no admission control and no eviction policy, so it renamed and trimmed the document without changing its job. Two of its three surviving checks (show the shape; guard against over-terseness) are the growth engines this design cuts. Take it as starting text and a source of recovered reasoning; the four-part structure above still governs.
**Files:** rename `resources/authoring-heuristic.md` → `resources/self-authoring-rules.md` and rewrite it (recovering the pre-revert version as the base); `CLAUDE.md` (the every-session pointer, plus the two existing references at Working conventions and Method docs); `AGENTS.md` (the same two references — it mirrors CLAUDE.md); `resources/method-compliance-audit-checklist.md` (references the old filename twice and re-uses its 4.8 checks as audit criteria — those criteria change); `resources/research/fable-5-instruction-compatibility.md` (has a section keyed to the old checklist). Host-only: the document is not in the plugin package and consumers never author method rules.
